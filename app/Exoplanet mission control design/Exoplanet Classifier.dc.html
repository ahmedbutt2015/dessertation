Glossary — Key Terms
KOI — Kepler Object of Interest
A star flagged by the Kepler telescope as showing a brightness dip that could be caused by an orbiting planet. There are 9,564 KOIs; each has an ID like K00001.01.
CONFIRMED
NASA has verified this signal is a genuine exoplanet using multiple lines of evidence (radial velocity, transit timing, statistical validation). Cannot be explained by a false alarm.
FALSE POSITIVE
The brightness dip is NOT a planet. Common causes: an eclipsing binary star behind the target, a background star with its own eclipse, or a systematic instrument artefact.
Orbital Period
How long it takes the planet to complete one full orbit around its host star, measured in days. Earth's period is 365 days. Hot Jupiters can orbit in under 2 days.
Planet Radius (R⊕)
Size of the planet expressed as multiples of Earth's radius. R⊕ = 1 is Earth-sized. Values around 1–2 R⊕ are rocky planets; above ~4 R⊕ are gas giants.
Transit Depth (ppm)
How much the star's brightness drops when the planet passes in front of it, in parts per million. Earth transiting the Sun causes ~84 ppm. Larger planets cause deeper dips.
GAF — Gramian Angular Field
A technique to convert a 1D light curve into a 2D image by encoding the angular relationships between time-series values. This lets a vision model "see" the transit shape.
ViT — Vision Transformer
A neural network architecture (ViT-B/16) that splits an image into 16×16 patches and learns relationships between them using attention. Originally designed for ImageNet; adapted here for GAF images.
LoRA — Low-Rank Adaptation
A parameter-efficient fine-tuning method. Instead of retraining all 86M ViT weights, LoRA injects small trainable matrices (rank r=16, ~590K params — 0.68% of the model) into the frozen backbone.
FAISS & RAG
FAISS is a vector similarity search library. RAG (Retrieval-Augmented Generation) retrieves the 5 most similar confirmed planets from a knowledge base of 2,745 systems to ground the AI explanation in real data.
